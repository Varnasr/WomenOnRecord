Data and method

Where the numbers come from, and how to use them

The data, free to use

The files below hold every number on this site, with the NCRB table it came from, the section of law it falls under, and a note wherever a figure needs one. You may use them freely if you cite NCRB and link to this site.

  • Excel workbookThe workbook has an About sheet, a codebook, the sources, every row of data, and one sheet for each crime with states in rows and years in columns.
  • CSVThe CSV has one row for each state, year, crime and measure, and holds the same rows as the workbook.
  • CodebookThe codebook explains every column, crime code, measure and flag.
  • Context workbookEight other sources, from the household survey to election affidavits, one sheet each, with the source, table and page on every row.
  • Context CSVThe same rows as the context workbook, in one file, with a column that names the source.

The figures are NCRB's. The compilation, the codes and the notes are released under CC BY 4.0, and the code is released under the MIT licence.

Read this before quoting a number

  1. These figures count the cases that police recorded, which is a smaller number than the crimes that took place. The National Family Health Survey asks women directly, and in its 2015-16 round 77 per cent of women who had experienced physical or sexual violence had never sought help or told anyone about it (NFHS-4 India report, p. 572).
  2. NCRB counts each FIR once, under the most serious offence in it. A rape that ended in a murder is therefore counted once, as murder with rape.
  3. Rape of girls under 18 is mostly booked under the POCSO Act. The rape figures and the POCSO figures are separate series and should be read together.
  4. From 1 July 2024 offences were booked under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. NCRB reports 2024 under both codes, and this site adds the two together. Where NCRB split one older heading into several, the parts are added back so that the five years can be compared, and each such sum is named in the dataset.
  5. Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu merged in January 2020. Ladakh became a union territory in 2019. All five years use today's 36 states and union territories.

Sources and method

Where every number comes from

National Crime Records Bureau, Crime in India, Volume 1, for 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024: tables 2A.3 (murder victims by sex), 3A.1 to 3A.3 (crimes against women and rape victims by age), 3A.5 to 3A.8 (police and court disposal).

    How it is checked

    A script read every table directly from NCRB's PDFs. For every count, the 28 states must add up to NCRB's own total for states, the 8 union territories must add up to its total for union territories, and the two together must equal the all-India total. They do in every table and every year. The yearly all-India totals in table 3A.2 were also matched against the totals NCRB prints in table 3A.1.

    No one is named

    This site holds counts only. It does not name, picture or describe any survivor, including in the cases that started the protests. Section 72 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, earlier section 228A of the IPC, makes disclosing a rape survivor's identity an offence.
