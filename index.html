    Each crime, five years, India

    Cases recorded each year. The first year is 2020, when the lockdown kept people from police stations and counts fell everywhere. In 2024 the new criminal laws came into force half way through the year, and the counts here add the cases booked under each.

    Read this before quoting a number

    1. These are cases recorded by police. They are not a count of how often these crimes happen. The National Family Health Survey asks women directly: in its 2015-16 round, 77 per cent of women who had experienced physical or sexual violence had never sought help or told anyone (NFHS-4 India report, p. 572).
    2. Each FIR is counted once, under its most serious offence. A rape that ended in murder is counted as murder with rape, not twice.
    3. Rape of girls under 18 is mostly booked under the POCSO Act. The rape figures and the POCSO figures are separate series and should be read together.
    4. From 1 July 2024 offences were booked under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. NCRB reports 2024 under both codes; this site adds them. Where NCRB split one older heading into several, the parts are added back so the five years compare. Each such sum is named in the dataset.
    5. Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu merged in January 2020. Ladakh became a union territory in 2019. All five years use today's 36 states and union territories.

    Sources and method

    Where every number comes from

    National Crime Records Bureau, Crime in India, Volume 1, for 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024: tables 2A.3 (murder victims by sex), 3A.1 to 3A.3 (crimes against women and rape victims by age), 3A.5 to 3A.8 (police and court disposal).

      How it is checked

      The tables were read from the PDFs by a script, not typed. For every count, the 28 states must add up to NCRB's own total for states, the 8 union territories to its total for union territories, and both to the all-India total. They do, in every table and every year. The figures headline numbers rest on were also compared with NCRB's published summaries.

      No one is named

      This site holds counts only. It does not name, picture or describe any survivor, including in the cases that started the protests. Section 72 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, earlier section 228A of the IPC, makes disclosing a rape survivor's identity an offence.
